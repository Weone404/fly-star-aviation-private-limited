## What is a Commercial Pilot License (CPL)?

A **Commercial Pilot License (CPL)** is an official pilot qualification issued by the **Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA)** in India. Holding a CPL authorizes you to fly aircraft commercially for remuneration—allowing you to work as an Airline First Officer, Charter Pilot, Cargo Pilot, or Flight Instructor.

To obtain a CPL in India, candidates must complete rigorous theoretical ground school training, clear all DGCA-mandated exams, pass Class 1 medical assessments, and log a minimum of **200 flight hours** at a recognized Flying Training Organization (FTO).

## CPL Eligibility Criteria in India

Before beginning your pilot training journey, candidates must meet the basic entry requirements set by the DGCA:

1. **Educational Qualification:** Must have passed 10+2 (or equivalent) with **Physics** and **Mathematics** from a recognized board. *(Note: Non-maths/science students can clear these subjects through NIOS open school).*
2. **Age Requirement:** Minimum **17 years** to start ground classes; **18 years** to obtain the CPL license.
3. **Medical Fitness:** Must possess a valid **DGCA Class 2 Medical Certificate** prior to flying training, followed by a **DGCA Class 1 Medical Certificate**.
4. **Language Proficiency:** Must be fluent in written and spoken English.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Q1. How many flight hours are required for a CPL in India?
To qualify for a DGCA Commercial Pilot License, candidates must log a minimum of **200 hours of flight time**, which includes solo flying, cross-country navigation, night flying, and instrument flight hours.

### Q2. Can a commerce or arts student become a commercial pilot in India?
Yes. Students without Physics and Mathematics in 10+2 can clear these two subjects independently through the **National Institute of Open Schooling (NIOS)** on-demand examinations while pursuing ground classes.

### Q3. How long does it take to complete a CPL course?
Typically, the CPL course takes **18 to 24 months**. Ground school and DGCA exams usually require 6 to 10 months, followed by 10 to 14 months of active flying training.

### Q4. What is RTR(A) and why is it necessary?
RTR(A) stands for **Radio Telephony Restricted (Aeronautical)**. Issued by the Wireless Planning and Coordination (WPC) wing of India, it licenses pilots to operate aircraft radio communication systems on aviation frequencies.
