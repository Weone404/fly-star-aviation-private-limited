§6 — REVIEW REPLIES
================================================================================
STATUS: [ ] not started  [ ] ongoing                     Updated: __________

WHY: replies are fresh, keyword-bearing text on a high-trust domain, written
by you. An unanswered review is a wasted asset. Reply to every review on
Google and Justdial, ideally within a week.

RULES
  - Never ask for a review in exchange for anything. Never write one yourself.
    Fake reviews breach India's Consumer Protection Act and platform policy.
  - Asking a real student to review you after they finish is fine and normal.
  - Vary the wording. Copy-pasted replies read as automated and add nothing.
  - Name the specific thing naturally: "the Air Navigation batch", "RTR
    preparation", "the Dwarka centre". That is the keyword-bearing part.

TEMPLATES — adapt, never paste verbatim

  Positive, specific:
    "Thank you, [name]. Glad the [Air Navigation / Meteorology] sessions
    worked for you — [instructor's approach / the mock tests] is something we
    put a lot into. All the best for your [DGCA paper / CPL]."

  Positive, generic:
    "Thanks [name], we appreciate it. Do let us know how the next paper goes."

  Negative, fixable:
    "Thank you for telling us, [name] — that is not the experience we want.
    [Specific thing you are changing.] Please call us on [canonical number] so
    we can sort it out directly."

  Negative, disputed:
    "We are sorry you feel that way. Our records show [factual, neutral
    statement]. We would like to discuss it — please reach us on [number]."
    Never argue publicly. Never disclose a student's personal details.

  Review that is not from a student:
    Reply factually and briefly, then report it to the platform if it breaches
    policy. Do not accuse anyone in the public reply.

ASKING FOR REVIEWS — the ethical pattern
  After a student passes a paper or finishes a batch, send one message:
  a thank-you, a link to the Google review page, and no incentive.
  One ask, no follow-up chasing.

DISCLOSURE IS MANDATORY
  Every answer that mentions or recommends Flying Star must say you run it.
  Quora removes undisclosed promotion, and readers spot it anyway.

ANSWER OUTLINES — the shape that gets upvoted and cited

  For "which ground classes are best" threads (1-4, 7):
    Do NOT lead with your own institute. Lead with how to choose:
      - Ask how many students per batch, and get the number in writing
      - Ask which subjects are taught by which instructor
      - Ask what happens if you fail a paper — is a repeat included?
      - Check whether the fee covers material and mock tests
      - Visit before paying; a centre that will not let you sit in one class
        is telling you something

    school in Dwarka, so take my view accordingly. Happy to answer specifics
    either way."

  For thread 5 (self-study vs ground school):
    Be honest that self-study works for disciplined candidates with good
    material, and say what a ground school actually adds — structure,
    doubt-clearing, mock discipline, syllabus updates. Someone who says
    "you cannot pass without us" is not credible. Disclose.

  For thread 6 (Technical General):
    Pure subject help. What the paper covers, which topics carry weight, what
    order to study in, what a realistic timeline looks like. No pitch at all.
    This is the answer that builds standing for the others.

  Wherever eligibility comes up, correct the PCM myth with the source. That
  correction is your most repeatable contribution — it is true, checkable,
  and most answers on Quora get it wrong.

RULES
  One account, your real name, real credentials in the bio. Answer 2-3
  questions a month properly rather than 20 badly. Never post the same text
  to multiple threads.
